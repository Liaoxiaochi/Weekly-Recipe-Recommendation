人们在选择食物时，为什么常常会陷入"健康-口味冲突（health-taste conflict）"或"选择惯性"？
The evidence is substantial across multiple studies. W. Stroebe et al., 2013 [1] presents a goal conflict model showing that when palatable food cues activate the eating enjoyment goal, they inhibit weight control goals, facilitating unhealthy eating. Xiaofei Li et al., 2024 [2] confirmed this conflict exists, finding that consumers perceive healthy foods as lacking tastiness and seek balance between both attributes rather than prioritizing health alone.
Regarding choice inertia, I. Contento et al., 2006 [3] found that adolescents (n=108) use familiarity/habit as a primary food choice criterion alongside taste and health. B. Briers et al., 2020 [4] demonstrated across six countries that consumers' belief that "tasty=unhealthy" reduces vegetable consumption and increases BMI, suggesting habitual reliance on this belief.
Additionally, Dissertation Johanna & Kruse (n.d.) [5] identifies this as an intertemporal choice problem: tasty foods provide immediate sensory reward while healthy diets offer delayed benefits, making resistance difficult.

现有的食谱推荐系统（特别是过度依赖协同过滤的系统）在处理**长期（如一周）**饮食计划时，存在什么致命缺陷？
Collaborative filtering-based recipe recommendation systems fail to account for dynamic metabolic changes and evolving preferences over extended periods, making them unsuitable for week-long dietary planning.
The available evidence is limited but directly relevant. M.Yasaswini et al. (2025) [1] explicitly identifies that "standard methods leave much to be desired when it comes to offering personalized, adaptable strategies aligned with our unique metabolic processes and changing preferences." The authors argue that CF systems treat users' past food preferences as static indicators, missing how "a user timeline, given all their past nutritional data, can still behave differently given food ordering tendencies." 
Additionally, Cao Xiao et al. (2019)[2] notes fundamental challenges in "translating noisy and dynamic behavioral data into personalized longitudinal planning," suggesting CF's reliance on historical patterns cannot capture the temporal dynamics required for week-long plans.
K. Kalantar-Zadeh & L. Moore (2020)[3] further emphasize that "a one-size-fits-all recipe is nonexistent" and that personalized approaches must account for individual physiological variation-a capability standard CF systems lack.

Evidence shows clear weekday-weekend distinctions: P. Ducrot et al., 2015[1] analyzed 53,025 participants and found that on weekdays, constraints (3.77) outweigh pleasure (3.42), while weekends reverse this pattern-pleasure (3.61) exceeds constraints (3.54). Organization matters more on weekdays (2.89) than weekends (2.75). Additionally, J. Santos et al., 2024[2] detected 7-day rhythmic patterns in food intake timing among urban residents, driven by work schedules, demonstrating that weekly cycles are structurally distinct rather than homogeneous. S. McCarthy, 2014[3] reports weekends show poorer dietary quality with higher fat and alcohol intake, reflecting different behavioral patterns. Sebastian Astroza et al., 2018[4] confirms significant inter-relationships between weekday and weekend activity scheduling, indicating that time allocation differs systematically across the week rather than treating all days identically.
